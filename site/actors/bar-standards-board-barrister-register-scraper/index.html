    Bar Standards Board Barrister Register Scraper is an Apify Actor published by Nimbryxo Studio. It accepts 19 input fields, and mode is required. Each result can carry up to 9 declared fields, including recordType, barristerName, currentPractisingStatus, memberStatus, dateOfCall and innOfCourt. It costs $0.99 per 1,000 results on Apify's Free plan and $0.49 on the Business plan.

    What it does

    Scrape the Bar Standards Board's official public register for England & Wales. Search the Barristers' Register by name or chambers, pull the complete authorised-practising register in a single run, browse past disciplinary findings with sanctions, costs and decision documents, check interim suspensions, export BSB-regulated entities, or fetch specific barrister and finding URLs directly. Everything comes from barstandardsboard.org.uk, the statutory regulator of barristers in England and Wales. No login, no cookies, no proxy configuration and no API key are needed.

    What this actor does

    • Six modes:
      • authorisedBarristers — the full authorised-practising register (about 18,700 barristers) from the BSB's own published CSV, with contact details, member status, reserved activities and any current disciplinary finding
      • search — live search of the Barristers' Register by barrister name or chambers/organisation name
      • disciplinaryFindings — browse the public register of past disciplinary findings, sorted by date or relevance
      • interimSuspensions — barristers currently subject to an interim suspension or interim conditions
      • entities — BSB-regulated legal-services businesses (authorised bodies, licensed bodies/ABS, and previously-authorised bodies), including their reserved legal activities
      • byUrl — fetch one or more barrister-register or disciplinary-finding URLs directly
    • Filters that match the regulator's own register: practising status, 28 areas of practice, Public Access registration, conduct-of-litigation authorisation, register view (practising vs. sanctioned non-practising)
    • Disciplinary filters: finding status, sanction type, hearing type, and a decision-date window (past month, past year, or a custom range)
    • Deep profiles: call date, Inn of Court, practising certificate validity, chambers address and switchboard, other addresses and numbers, rights of audience, areas of practice, other entitlements, youth-courts registration and disciplinary history
    • Decision documents: finding records include sanction, costs, status and links to published decision/report documents where the BSB provides them
    • Empty fields are always omitted — every value is real extracted data, never null, N/A or a placeholder sentence

    Use cases

    • Due diligence — verify a barrister's practising status, certificate validity, Inn of Court and disciplinary history before instructing them
    • Legal directories and comparison sites — import the full authorised-practising register with chambers, contact details and practice areas
    • Compliance monitoring — track new disciplinary findings, sanctions and interim suspensions as they are published
    • Recruitment and chambers research — size practice areas, identify Public Access or litigation-authorised barristers, and map chambers
    • Regulatory analytics — measure the distribution of practising statuses, areas of practice and reserved activities across England & Wales
    • Journalism and legal research — cross-reference a finding's sanction, costs and published decision documents with case reporting

    Questions

    Is this official data?

    Yes. Every record comes from the Bar Standards Board's own public register at barstandardsboard.org.uk, the statutory regulator for barristers in England and Wales.

    What is the difference between the search and authorisedBarristers modes?

    search asks the live register for a name or chambers match and can enrich each result from its profile page. authorisedBarristers downloads the BSB's published full-register CSV in one request and returns every authorised practising barrister with the complete CSV field set. Use the CSV mode for bulk work and search for targeted lookups.

    What is the difference between practising and sanctionedNonPractising?

    practising covers currently authorised barristers who hold a practising certificate. sanctionedNonPractising covers disbarred or unregistered barristers who have a current disciplinary finding.

    Why do some barrister records have fewer fields than others?

    The register only publishes certain details for certain statuses. For example, a disbarred barrister has no current chambers address, so that field is omitted rather than shown as empty.

    Does searchQuery support partial matches?

    Yes. The register performs a substring match on names or chambers/organisation names, the same as the site's own search box. The full-register mode applies the same partial-match rule to the CSV.

    Can I get every barrister in one run?

    Yes. Use mode: "authorisedBarristers" and set maxItems to 20000, the current maximum and above the register size (18,739 rows at the latest verification).

    How current is the register?

    The BSB updates the register continuously, with up to an hour of processing lag. The full-register CSV is regenerated by the BSB; sourceUrl identifies the current downloadable asset (the URL itself does not encode a snapshot date).

    Can I look up a barrister when I only have a URL?

    Yes. Use mode: "byUrl" with full barrister-register or disciplinary_finding URLs.

    Are decision documents always available?

    No. The BSB publishes a decision or report document for some findings only. When a document is published, it appears in documents; otherwise the field is omitted.

    Do I need to set up a proxy?

    No. The actor connects to the public register directly. Just fill in the input and run it.

    Limits and notes

    Limitations

    • The BSB's Disqualified persons page is currently empty ("There are currently no persons disqualified"), so it is not exposed as a mode. This is a currently-empty surface, not a proven unparseable page.
    • The full-register CSV does not publish a barrister profile URL or register ID. Those two fields are available in search and byUrl records, but not in authorisedBarristers records.
    • The register's own date filter is unreliable and can return the full findings list for any date selected. The actor therefore applies the decision-date window to each finding's actual decision date, so the filter works as expected.
    • Historical findings before 2002 are not published by the BSB.
    • Some addresses or phone numbers are genuinely unpublished by the BSB for a particular barrister; those fields are omitted rather than invented.
