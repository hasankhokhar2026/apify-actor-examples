    LinkedIn Post Comment & Reply Thread Extractor is an Apify Actor published by Nimbryxo Studio. It accepts 10 input fields, and postUrls and cookie are required. Each result can carry up to 7 declared fields, including authorName, commentText, commentedAt, reactionsCount, isReply and postUrl. It costs $0.99 per 1,000 results on Apify's Free plan and $0.49 on the Business plan.

    What it does

    Feed this actor one or more LinkedIn post links and a session cookie, and it returns the top-level comments on each post (up to your per-post limit) — optionally with nested reply threads — as structured rows with author identity, engagement counts, and linked profiles.

    What this actor does

    • Scrapes top-level comments from one or more LinkedIn post URLs, with a relevance or recency sort preference.
    • Optionally follows reply threads up to 3 levels deep, emitting each reply as its own row linked to its parent via parentCommentId.
    • Classifies each commenter as a person or company page, and pulls any LinkedIn profile or company links out of the comment text.
    • Filters out comments below a minimum reaction count, and can exclude comments from person or company authors entirely.
    • Omits text and URL fields it couldn't populate for a given comment; the counts (reactionsCount, repliesCount, authorFollowersCount), isReply and authorType are always present.

    Use cases

    • Audience sentiment — read how people are reacting to a company announcement or thought-leadership post.
    • Lead discovery — surface engaged commenters as warm prospects, filtered to real people by excludeAuthorTypes.
    • Influencer research — see who's actively replying to a creator's posts and how deep the conversation threads go.
    • Brand monitoring — track mentions of your company or product inside comment threads on relevant posts.
    • Community moderation review — pull full reply chains to review how a sensitive post's discussion unfolded.

    Questions

    Does this need a LinkedIn account?

    Yes. A valid session cookie (li_at or an exported cookies JSON array) from a logged-in account is required to read comment data.

    Why would authorName be missing from a comment?

    Deleted accounts or heavily restricted profiles can leave author fields empty. If a comment has no extractable ID at all, it's skipped entirely.

    Does the actor see deleted comments?

    No. Deleted comments are removed from LinkedIn's own response, so the actor only sees whatever is still live at scrape time.

    How deep do reply threads go?

    Up to 3 levels via replyDepth, only when includeReplies is enabled. At each level the actor reads up to 10 replies per comment, and replies beyond the chosen depth aren't fetched.

    Why did I get fewer comments than maxCommentsPerPost?

    The post may simply have fewer comments, minReactions/excludeAuthorTypes may have dropped some, or LinkedIn may serve that post's comments in a way that only exposes its first batch. Comments are not de-duplicated across different input URLs pointing to the same post.

    Can I scrape comments on a post I don't have access to?

    No. Only posts visible to the account behind your cookie can be scraped — private or connection-restricted posts outside your network will fail.

    Why is a cookie required for public-looking posts?

    LinkedIn's comment data isn't available through any unauthenticated public endpoint, even for posts that appear public in a browser without logging in.

    What happens if my cookie expires mid-run?

    The run stops and adds a record with _status: "no_data" plus a _reason/_message/_help explanation; any comment rows saved before that point remain. Log into LinkedIn, copy a fresh li_at value, and start a new run.

    Is this affiliated with LinkedIn?

    No. This is an independent tool and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by LinkedIn Corporation.
