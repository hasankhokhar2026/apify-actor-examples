    Dallas TX Building Permit Records & Contractor Lookup is an Apify Actor published by Nimbryxo Studio. It accepts 27 input fields, and mode is required. Each result can carry up to 14 declared fields, including permitNumber, permitType, activity, status, issuedDate and createdDate. It costs $0.99 per 1,000 results on Apify's Free plan and $0.49 on the Business plan.

    What it does

    Dallas publishes two different building-permit archives: a live permit feed that is updated daily, and a large historical GIS extract that reaches back to 1971. This actor reads both, so you can work with current contractor leads and decades of permit history in one place.

    What this actor does

    • Searches live permits by permit type, land use, ZIP code, contractor text, Mapsco grid, work-description text, street-address text, issue-date range, declared value, and area.
    • Searches historical permits by coarse permit type, activity, status, category, council district, ZIP code, land-use text, work-description text, street-address text, issue-date range, value, and area.
    • Sorts live results by value, area, or issue date, and historical results by issue date, application date, value, area, or units.
    • Looks up exact permit numbers across both sources and merges their fields, so a historical permit can gain status and coordinates while a live permit keeps its contractor information.
    • Offers complete dropdowns for every finite value published by each source, plus contains-match overrides where a field has too many values for a practical dropdown.
    • Omits any field the original filing never recorded instead of emitting a placeholder.
    • Requires no login, cookie, API key, or proxy setup.

    Use cases

    • Contractor lead generation - find live permits with a named contractor for the trades and neighborhoods you serve.
    • Neighborhood development research - compare recent filings with historical construction patterns by ZIP, council district, or land use.
    • Property due diligence - check whether a specific address or permit number appears in either Dallas archive.
    • Market sizing - total declared permit value, area, or dwelling units across a type, category, or district.
    • Civic and academic research - study five decades of Dallas permitting, including final status and geographic distribution.

    Limits and notes

    Two complementary permit sources

    Source Coverage Best for
    City of Dallas Open Data (live) Permits issued from January 2018 to today; updated daily Contractor names and phone numbers, current filings, ZIP-level lead lists
    City of Dallas GIS archive Permits created 1971-02-01 through 2024-11-12 Long-run research, permit status, council districts, units, zoning, map coordinates

    Live records and historical records share the same core fields. Historical records add status, activity, category, council district, units, land-use code, zoning, and latitude/longitude. Live records add contractor details and Mapsco grid references. Permit-number lookups check both sources and merge the two records when the same permit exists in each.

    Data notes

    How current is the live data? The City of Dallas Open Data feed is updated daily and currently holds permits issued from January 2018 onward. The record count changes as new permits are added.

    How current is the historical data? The GIS archive was extracted from the City's permitting system on 2024-11-12 and is not updated. It covers permits created from 1971-02-01 through that date. The archive also contains unissued applications that never received an issue date: they are returned when your status or permit-type filter selects them, sort after issued permits on issue-date sorts, and are omitted when you filter by an issue-date range.

    Why do some historical records have no issuedDate? The GIS archive includes applications that were still in review, rejected, withdrawn, or otherwise never issued when the City extracted the layer. These rows carry their real status but no issue date, so the field is omitted instead of being shown as a placeholder.

    Why do some fields appear on only some records? The two archives publish different field sets. Contractor names, contractor phones, and Mapsco grids exist only in the live feed. Status, activity, category, council district, units, land-use code, zoning, and coordinates exist only in the historical layer. Fields are omitted when the source has no value.

    Why do some records have no value or area? Many alteration and trade permits never had a declared dollar value or square footage. A recorded zero is treated as "not recorded", so the field is left off rather than shown as a misleading 0.

    Why does ascending value/area sorting skip zero-valued rows? A large share of permits have no recorded value or area. Sorting ascending without a floor would put every not-recorded permit first. The ascending sort therefore starts at 1, while descending sorts naturally leave those records at the bottom.

    What happens when a permit exists in both archives? Permit-number lookups use the live record as the primary record and fill any missing fields from the historical record. Shared fields keep the live value; historical-only fields such as status, councilDistrict, and latitude are added.

    Are the coordinates available for live permits? No. The live open-data feed publishes only a street address, ZIP code, and Mapsco grid. Coordinates come from the historical GIS layer.

    How many records can one run return? One run returns at most maxItems records (maximum 10,000). The live feed currently holds about 397,000 permits and the historical archive about 1.8 million, so a broad query can match far more than one run can emit. To collect a larger set, split the query into smaller date ranges, permit types, council districts, or ZIP codes and combine the datasets. Capped runs of 1,000 or more set a status message so a full run is never mistaken for an exhausted source.

    Why do seven ZIP codes return no historical permits? The live feed contains seven ZIP codes that have no clean rows in the 1971-2024 GIS archive: 75024, 75039, 75052, 75104, 75141, 75180, and 75270. They are missing from that snapshot (four appear only inside malformed combined ZIP values), so a historical search on one of them returns 0 records by design. The same ZIPs work in live mode.

    Does the historical archive include applications that were never issued? Yes. About 154,000 of the 1.8 million historical rows have no issue date because they were cancelled, rejected, still in review, or otherwise not issued when the City extracted the archive. They are returned when a status, activity, or permit-type filter selects them, sort last on issue-date sorts, and are omitted when an issue-date range is set. A small number of rows whose status is Issued also lack an issue date in the City's extract; the actor keeps the source status and omits the missing date rather than inventing one.

    Is this affiliated with the City of Dallas? No. This actor reads public City of Dallas data and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to the City of Dallas.
