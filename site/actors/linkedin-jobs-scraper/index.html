    LinkedIn Job Listings with Salary & Company Data Scraper is an Apify Actor published by Nimbryxo Studio. It accepts 29 input fields, none of them required at the top level. Each result can carry up to 22 declared fields, including title, companyName, location, salary, salarySource and applicantCount. It costs $0.99 per 1,000 results on Apify's Free plan and $0.49 on the Business plan.

    What it does

    Turn LinkedIn's public job board into a clean spreadsheet. Search by keywords, locations and employers, or paste job links, and get one row per posting with the full description, a yearly salary range with its source, applicant numbers, Easy Apply status, seniority, the recruiter when LinkedIn shows one, and the employer's LinkedIn profile. No LinkedIn account, login or cookies needed.

    What this actor does

    • Salary you can sort by. Pay is parsed into minimum, maximum, currency and period, then converted to yearly figures. It comes from LinkedIn's own pay box or, when the employer only wrote it in the description, from that text. salarySource tells you which.
    • Many searches in one run. Several keywords times several locations, company-only searches, pasted LinkedIn search links and individual job links, merged without duplicates.
    • Filters that really narrow the results. Date posted (from the past hour to the past 3 months), distance, Easy Apply, fewer than 10 applicants, companies to include or exclude, experience level, job type, industry, staffing agencies, salary and recruiter.
    • Employer profile included. Industry, size, headquarters, website, founding year, office addresses, follower count, tagline and description, looked up once per company.
    • Empty fields are omitted. Fields LinkedIn doesn't show for a posting are left out instead of filled with blanks or guesses.

    Use cases

    • Salary benchmarking: compare yearly pay for a title across cities, countries and employers.
    • Recruiting and sourcing: watch fresh postings in your niche, see who is hiring and who posted each role.
    • Sales prospecting: spot companies that are hiring fast in a segment, with their size, website and offices.
    • Job hunting: a daily run with "Past 24 hours", Easy Apply and fewer than 10 applicants, skipping jobs you've already seen.
    • Labour-market research: track demand, seniority mix and pay transparency over time.

    Questions

    Do I need a LinkedIn account or cookies?

    No. The actor reads the job pages LinkedIn shows to signed-out visitors.

    Can I filter for remote, hybrid or on-site jobs?

    Neither the public search nor the job page shows the remote/hybrid/on-site label to signed-out visitors, and the search ignores the workplace filter, so a remote filter can't be offered. remoteAllowed is true when the title or description clearly says the role is fully remote, or when the job page includes LinkedIn's structured data marking it TELECOMMUTE. That structured block only ever marks remote jobs, never hybrid or on-site, so neither is inferred; not every remote job carries it, so the field can be missing for a remote role. Adding "remote" to your keywords narrows results to jobs that mention it. Older saved inputs that still contain the remote filter or the job-function filter keep running; those two settings are ignored and the run's status message says so.

    Why do some jobs have no salary?

    Many employers don't publish pay. The actor reads LinkedIn's pay box and pay ranges written in the description, and never estimates. salarySource shows where each figure came from. Expect most US jobs in pay-transparency states to have a salary, and far fewer elsewhere.

    How do I get more than 1,000 jobs?

    LinkedIn returns at most 1,000 jobs for one search. When a search reaches that limit, the actor automatically continues with the same search restricted to the past month, week, 3 days and 24 hours. Each of those returns its own results, and duplicates are removed, so one keyword and location can yield well over 1,000 jobs. For even more, add locations, keywords or companies. Turn this off with splitLargeSearches if you only want LinkedIn's top 1,000.

    Why can't I get the employer's own application link?

    LinkedIn only shows the external "Apply on company website" link to signed-in members. applyUrl is the LinkedIn page where the application starts, and easyApply tells you whether you can apply on LinkedIn directly.

    My location returned the wrong country. LinkedIn quietly searches the whole United States when it doesn't recognise a place. The actor checks every location first: an unrecognised one is matched to LinkedIn's closest suggestion or skipped with a note in the status message. Type places in English ("Germany", not "Deutschland"), or use geoIds.

    Which details does LinkedIn hide from signed-out visitors?

    Remote/hybrid/on-site labels, the employer's own application link, whether a job was reposted, view counts and the job-function category are only shown to signed-in members, so this actor can't return them. Expiry dates are partly available: when the job page carries structured data, expiresAt is LinkedIn's own expiry time. Workarounds: add "remote" to your keywords or use includeTitleKeywords with "remote" or "hybrid"; use remoteAllowed for jobs that clearly state they're fully remote or whose structured data marks them TELECOMMUTE; use postedDate (the latest posting date) for freshness; to track reposts and closures yourself, run on a schedule and compare id values between runs. The exact posting time (postedAt) appears only on some versions of a job page. Extended job page depth asks again to fill it for most jobs, but not all; postedDate is always filled.

    How do I get only new jobs on a schedule?

    Use "Date posted: Past 24 hours", or keep the id values from earlier runs and pass them in excludeJobIds.

    Do I need to set up a proxy?

    No. The actor picks its connection automatically and switches when LinkedIn slows it down.

    Is this affiliated with LinkedIn?

    No. It is an independent tool that reads LinkedIn's public job pages.
